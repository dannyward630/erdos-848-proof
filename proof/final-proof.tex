\begin{abstract}
We prove that, for every positive integer $N$, the largest
$A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ for which $ab+1$ is nonsquarefree for all
$a,b\in A$, including $a=b$, has cardinality
$\lfloor(N+18)/25\rfloor$.  The proof combines an elementary lower
construction, an independently checked exact coloring certificate through
$100000006$, exact-rational structural interval certificates through
$264000000000000000$, and an independently audited explicit high-range
theorem.  We state the certificate semantics and trusted execution boundary
explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and sharpness}

For positive $N$, call $A\subseteq[N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}$ \emph{admissible}
when $ab+1$ is nonsquarefree for all ordered pairs $a,b\in A$, including
the diagonal.  Let $f(N)$ be the maximum cardinality of such a set and set
\[
  \Bseven(N)=\left\lfloor\frac{N+18}{25}\right\rfloor.
\]

\begin{theorem}
For every positive integer $N$,
\[
  f(N)=\Bseven(N).
\]
\end{theorem}

The theorem is the exact source-audited statement; it makes no uniqueness
claim.  The residue class
\[
  A_7(N)=\{a\in[N]:a\equiv7\pmod{25}\}
\]
is admissible, since
$ab+1\equiv7^2+1=50\equiv0\pmod{25}$ for
$a,b\equiv7\pmod{25}$.  Writing
$N=25q+r$, $0\le r<25$, shows
$|A_7(N)|=q+\mathbf1_{r\ge7}=\Bseven(N)$.  Hence
$f(N)\ge\Bseven(N)$.

\section{Graph reduction}

Let
\[
 D_N=\{a\in[N]:a^2+1\text{ is nonsquarefree}\}.
\]
Form $G_N$ on $D_N$, joining distinct $a,b$ when $ab+1$ is
nonsquarefree.  The diagonal condition places every admissible element in
$D_N$, and the remaining conditions say exactly that the set is a clique.
Thus $f(N)=\omega(G_N)$.  A proper coloring of $G_N$ has squarefree
products plus one within each color class, so
\[
 \chi(G_N)\le\Bseven(N)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 f(N)\le\Bseven(N).
\]

\section{Exact finite theorem}

Put $e_i=25i+6$.  Since $\Bseven(N)=i$ for
$25i-18\le N\le25i+6$, an $i$-coloring at $e_i$ restricts to every
prefix in that block.  There are no diagonal-eligible vertices below $7$.

\subsection{Diagonal completeness}

If $p^2\mid a^2+1$, then $p\ne2$, $p\equiv1\pmod4$, and
$p^2\le a^2+1<(a+1)^2$, so $p\le a$.  For every prime
$p\le100000006$ with $p\equiv1\pmod4$, the independent checker obtains
both roots of $-1$ modulo $p$ by Tonelli--Shanks and uniquely Hensel-lifts
them modulo $p^2$.  Marking the two progressions therefore reconstructs
exactly all diagonal vertices.  The count is $10515898$.

\subsection{Endpoint induction}

The base stream supplies all $4000$ endpoint states through $100006$.
At endpoint $e_i$ the checker colors every diagonal vertex through $e_i$ with
a color below $i$ and checks every same-color pair.  The compact stream supplies
$3996000$ later transitions.  Each transition introduces the two principal
anchors, performs no swap or one old-bin swap of an $18$-anchor, and places
the exact newly exposed outsiders.  Every changed bin is checked; unchanged
bins retain the induction invariant.  Hence every endpoint through
$e_{4000000}=100000006$ has the required coloring.

The exhaustive replay checked $18049789$ base pair occurrences.
It also checked all $14458371$ compact affected-bin pair occurrences,
together with $1379312$ swaps and $2513387$ placements.

\subsection{Exact factor leaves}

Every accepted pair value is factored by small-prime division and
deterministic Brent splitting.  The checker verifies the exact factor product
and absence of repeated factors and exports every terminal leaf used by an
accepted computation.  Miller--Rabin only proposes terminal leaves; all such
leaves are independently certified below.

There are $11108162$ distinct leaves, with maximum
$m=9999932500113877$ and $L=\lfloor\sqrt m\rfloor=99999662$.
An exact sieve proves the $2328675$ leaves at most $L$ prime.  Let $M$ be
the primorial through $L$ and $P$ the product of the $8779487$ larger
leaves.  Exact GMP arithmetic gives $\gcd(P,M)=1$.  A composite large leaf
would have a prime divisor at most $L$, contradicting this gcd.  Thus all
leaves are prime, and every accepted pair value is squarefree.

The canonical leaf receipt has SHA-256
\sha{4fc75b0ed263df81c07c5997c915511351ea61fff085d3ac95159c524ca9aad6}.
The endpoint-401 mutation temporarily puts $18$ with $382$, for which
$18\cdot382+1=13\cdot23^2$, and repairs the later state.  The exhaustive
checker rejects it at endpoint 401; sampled checking does not.  Therefore
\[
f(N)\le\Bseven(N)\qquad(1\le N\le100000006).
\]

\section{Structural reduction}

Fix a positive integer $N$ and put
\[
 A_{18}(N)=\{a\in[N]:a\equiv18\pmod{25}\},\qquad
 O_N=D_N\setminus\bigl(A_7(N)\cup A_{18}(N)\bigr).
\]
The roots of $-1$ modulo $25$ are $7$ and $18$; call the elements of $O_N$
outsiders.  For $z\in O_N$, let
\[
 w(z)=\min\{p:p\text{ prime and }p^2\mid z^2+1\}.
\]
An outsider has witness $13$ or at least $17$.  For a possible least
witness $p$, write $W_{p,N}=\{z\in O_N:w(z)=p\}$ and
$U_{>p,N}=\bigcup_{q>p}W_{q,N}$, with $q$ prime.  For $z\in W_{p,N}$, put
\[
 L_{z,N}=\{x\in A_7(N):zx+1\text{ is nonsquarefree}\},\qquad
 R_{z,N}=\{y\in A_{18}(N):zy+1\text{ is nonsquarefree}\}.
\]
Join $x\in L_{z,N}$ and $y\in R_{z,N}$ when $xy+1$ is squarefree,
obtaining a bipartite graph $H_{z,N}$.  Define
\[
 s_N(z)=|L_{z,N}|+|R_{z,N}|-\nu(H_{z,N}),\qquad
 c_{>p,N}(z)=|\{u\in U_{>p,N}:zu+1\text{ is nonsquarefree}\}|,
\]
and $g_{p,N}(z)=s_N(z)+c_{>p,N}(z)$.

\begin{lemma}[Structural rank]
If $A\subseteq[N]$ is admissible and has globally least outsider witness $p$,
then
\[
 |A|\le\max_{1\le t\le|W_{p,N}|}
       \bigl(t+g_{p,N}^\downarrow(t)\bigr),
\]
where the scores are ordered decreasingly.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $T=A\cap W_{p,N}$ and $t=|T|$.  For $z\in T$, the principal part of
$A$ is an independent set in $H_{z,N}$, hence has size at most $s_N(z)$ by
K\H{o}nig's theorem.  Every higher-witness outsider in $A$ is counted by
$c_{>p,N}(z)$.  Thus
$|A|\le t+\min_{z\in T}g_{p,N}(z)$.  The minimum on any $t$-element set is
at most its $t$-th largest ambient score.
\end{proof}

This gives three exhaustive branches: no outsider, least witness $13$, and
least witness at least $17$.  The no-outsider branch is the corresponding
principal bipartite independent-set bound.

\section{Exact structural interval certificates}

Each certificate row is a closed interval $[A,B]$.  Positive census,
collision, and Pell terms are evaluated at a uniform upper endpoint;
subtractive gains are evaluated at their weakest endpoint; and translation
remainders are maximized over the complete $25$-cycle.  The resulting exact
integer or rational is compared with $\Bseven(A)$.  Since $\Bseven$ is
nondecreasing, an accepted row proves every integer in its interval.

The independently audited envelope inputs are exact residue censuses and CRT
counts, the $p=17$ orientation cap, the $p\ge29$ domination bound, the
no-outsider two-degree bound, the rank lemma above, and a generalized-Pell
orbit bound.  For the latter, solutions are grouped into ideals of norm
dividing the fixed discriminant; two generators of one ideal differ by a
totally positive norm-one unit.  Such a nontrivial unit has trace at least
$3$, so successive positive solutions grow by more than
$(3+\sqrt5)/2>2$.  Every prime-count call satisfies the hypothesis of
Hanson's strict bound $\pi(x)<1.25506x/\log x$; the computation replaces
the logarithm by a weaker exact-rational lower bound.

We make explicit one correction to the wording of the pinned
\path{P13_RANK_ENVELOPE.md}.  For a translated pair $x,x+d\in[N]$, put
$\Delta=d^2-4$.  If $x(x+d)+1=m\ell^2$ with $\ell>Y$, then
$x(x+d)+1\le N^2+1<2N^2$, so $m<2N^2/Y^2$.  The ideal-orbit bound gives at
most $\tau(|\Delta|)K(B)$ solutions for each fixed $m$, where
$K(B)=1+\lceil\log_2(4B)\rceil$.  Hence the absolute count at the actual
$N$ is at most
\[
 \frac{2\tau(|\Delta|)K(B)N^2}{Y^2}.
\]
After division by $M=N/25$, this is
\[
 \frac{50\tau(|\Delta|)K(B)N}{Y^2}
 \le \frac{50\tau(|\Delta|)K(B)B}{Y^2},
\]
exactly the term used by the verifier.  The upstream prose instead passed
from an absolute $B^2$ bound directly to the last display, which does not
follow; the actual-$N$ derivation above is the required repair and changes no
certificate value.

\section{Explicit high range and conclusion}

Sothanaphan's independently audited explicit theorem proves the same upper
bound for every $N\ge264000000000000000$.  Its exact-rational numerical
claims have digest
\sha{4726814d80fc63353a77c18bac8691bc917efd5ef1c7dcf10a14aed03674b215}.
For clarity, put
\[
C_{\mathrm{quad}}=1-
 \prod_{\substack{p\equiv1\pmod4\\p\ge13}}
 \left(1-\frac2{p^2}\right).
\]
The corrected form of the note's misprinted Corollary 2 is the following:
if $A\subseteq[N]$ is admissible, $N\ge264000000000000000$, and
$A^*=A\setminus(A_7(N)\cup A_{18}(N))$, then
\[
 \frac{|A^*|}{N}\le\frac{23}{25}C_{\mathrm{quad}}+0.001973;
\]
if $A^*$ consists only of odd elements, then
\[
 \frac{|A^*|}{N}\le\frac{23}{50}C_{\mathrm{quad}}+0.001973.
\]
Indeed, admissibility on $(x,x)$ gives $\mu(x^2+1)=0$ for every
$x\in A^*$.  Apply Proposition 2 of the note to the 23 nonprincipal
classes modulo 25, or to their 23 odd lifts modulo 50, writing $q=25$ or
$50$, respectively, and taking $R=\lceil(259/200)N^{2/3}\rceil$.  The main terms sum to at most
$23(N/q+1)C_{\mathrm{quad}}$.  The pinned exact monotonicity audit bounds
the remaining normalized error per class by $8.577\times10^{-5}$ at the
threshold and thereafter, while
$23C_{\mathrm{quad}}/N<3\times10^{-18}$.  Summing and rounding upward gives
$0.001973$.  This proves the corrected corollary and identifies the diagonal
step for which admissibility is required.  Every invocation in Theorem 1
supplies that hypothesis.

The finite interval $[1,100000006]$ overlaps the first structural interval
$[100000000,10^9]$.  The remaining ranges share the endpoints $10^9$,
$10^{12}$, and $264000000000000000$.  Their union is every positive
integer.  Hence $f(N)\le\Bseven(N)$ for all positive $N$; the residue
class construction gives the reverse inequality and proves the theorem.

\section{Assurance boundary}

This is a computer-assisted proof using exact integer or rational arithmetic,
authenticated certificate bytes, complete interval coverage, independent
finite checking, batch primality certificates, and negative controls.
Numerical evidence alone is not used as proof.  Reproduction commands and
decisive hashes are in \texttt{REPRODUCE.md}; complete run identities and
receipts are in \texttt{certificates/}.

The research, implementation, drafting, and separated review lanes were
carried out in a multi-agent OpenAI Codex workflow under Danny Ward's
direction.  Here ``independent'' means a distinct implementation or internal
review assignment, not external scholarly peer review.  This public proof
candidate is unrefereed; full provenance is in \texttt{PROVENANCE.md}.

The mathematical proof does not depend on the separate ART-006 Lean artifact.
ART-006's own publication state reports a closed kernel theorem with axiom
closure drawn only from
\[

 \texttt{Classical.choice},\qquad

\]
Our source audit found that interface coherent and noncircular.  This is
strong upstream evidence, but not the independent clean rebuild required
here.  The repository's stricter completion protocol keeps formal
certification open until a sufficiently provisioned Windows host builds the
exact 30636-module provider from source, performs trust-zero replay, and
captures the live \texttt{\#print axioms} and dependency audit.
